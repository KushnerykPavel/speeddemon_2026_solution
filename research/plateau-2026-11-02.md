## 1. Problem reconstructed from the spec

- **F** 1,740,512 nodes, 5,000,000 edge lines, 2,000 queries. The whole process is timed (exec → exit).
  K = (Tref/T)^0.5 · (Mref/M)^0.2 · (Sref/S)^0.15 · (Dref/D)^0.15. Final score: hidden seed, 5 runs, middle 3.
- **F** The graph is a jittered lattice road network with arterials every 8th row/column and highways every 48th.
  Ids are randomly relabelled and lines are shuffled, so locality has to be recovered at run time.
- **F** A normal query's target is the r-th vertex a Dijkstra from s settles, with log2 r uniform on [1, 17].
  Median r = 512, mean ≈ 12,300, max 131,071. **D** A unidirectional search settles about Σr ≈ 1,950 × 12,300
  ≈ 24M vertices. Our bidirectional search pops 7.57M (**M**, step 25), about 1/3 of that. Search work is set
  by the query generator, not by the graph size.
- **M (new census)** After dropping 508 self-loops and collapsing 6,016 parallel or duplicate lines,
  **97.12% of directed neighbour pairs also have the reverse edge**. 144,060 pairs are one-way.
  One entry per neighbour per row, carrying both directions, gives **5,137,536 row entries**. Today we store
  10,000,000 (5M forward + 5M reverse). 99.999% of weights are < 2^12 (p99.9 = 1,949); the ≈50 above are the
  injected near-2^32 weights.
- **M (new census)** Merged row length histogram (0..8): 17 / 101,147 / 441,141 / 701,013 / 444,748 / 44,695 /
  6,491 / 506 / 676. 400,452 vertices have a merged row of 2 entries, both two-way: the degree-2 bypass set,
  found with no in-edge pass.

## 2. The current performance model, challenged

### What the model assumes, and how firm each part is

| Assumption | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| VPS (6-vCPU Haswell KVM) gains predict board gains | **Falsified twice** | SWAR parser −15% on the VPS, nothing on the board. THP-dense −4.7/−7.1% on the VPS (17/20 pairs), +3.7% on the board (3 samples vs 21). **M** |
| The board split is ≈55% build / 45% search | **Borrowed, not ours** | It comes from FlyHigh's exit-code builds on the box plus our Mac and VPS. No board phase timing of our binary exists. **U** |
| Search is latency-bound | Supported | ≈90 ns/pop at 1 thread on the Mac. Prefetch gave −7 to −13%. No renumbering costs +130%. **M** |
| "The queue is closed" | True for queue arithmetic only | Five queue variants lost, all with one expansion in flight. Nothing has tested more outstanding misses. **D** |
| The reverse graph has to be a second CSR | **Never questioned** | The census shows 97% of it duplicates the forward adjacency. **M** |
| OS memory costs are small | Weakly supported | VPS: sys 0.24 of 2.42 CPU-s, 48k minor faults. But E1 cut faults 24% and saved 5–7% of VPS wall time. Board share unknown. **M/U** |
| Our gap to the leaders is algorithmic | **Untested** | Cache Me (0.168 s) uses the common two-graph pipeline (Nov 1 report; verify in their repo). FlyHigh (0.151 s) is the same pipeline **plus** approach A (no reverse CSR) **plus** THP on for the dense arrays. Both use bidirectional Dijkstra and BFS regions, and both beat our 0.207 s mean by 19–35%. **F** |

The last row matters most. Three binaries with known board times form a ladder: ours (0.207 s), Cache Me as the
same-architecture control (0.168 s), and FlyHigh as the "A + selective THP" treatment (0.151 s). The board ratios are
Cache Me/ours ≈ 0.81 and FlyHigh/Cache Me ≈ 0.90. Running all three on our machines separates two questions: does
the VPS reproduce the control ratio (is our proxy faithful for code differences?), and does it reproduce the
treatment ratio (is A's effect visible off the box?). The cost is about one hour (Experiment 0).

### Roofline and Amdahl (deductions)

- **D** Edge-array passes per run: parse (92 MB of text) → bucket write (40 MB) → place → BFS read → copy with
  head translation → transpose count (5M atomic increments, random) → transpose scatter (5M random writes).
  That is about 7 passes over 5M edges, 3 of them random-access. The search does about 7.57M pops × ≈2.9 edges ≈
  22M local relaxations, comparable to 4–5 edge passes. **By operation count, removing one random pass equals
  a 20–25% search cut.** In time it is likely worth more, because a random-pass operation misses more often
  than a local relaxation.
- **D** Nothing runs near a bandwidth roof. Parsing runs at about 0.5 GB/s per thread (92 MB / 12 threads /
  15 ms on the Mac). The search moves about 1.5 GB in 68 ms at 12 threads, ≈10% of M1 Pro bandwidth. Every phase
  is bound by latency or instruction count, so the 2× the leaders show is physically available.
- **D** The board's L3 is 24 MB. The search structure is ≈54 MB (forward and reverse CSR with offsets) plus labels,
  so every query's cold misses go to DRAM. Any representation that removes bytes helps the board more than the Mac,
  whose larger SLC and L2 mask it.
- **D** Ceiling per direction, using board ≈0.207 s and an assumed 0.11 s build / 0.09 s search split (**S**):
  removing the transpose (≈10% on the Mac, 12% on the VPS) and cutting search 20% gives about −18%. Doubling
  search MLP gives at most −22%. Removing all fault and spawn overhead gives about −5 to −10%.

### TRIZ contradictions

1. *Backward search needs in-edges* ↔ *building and holding a second graph costs a random scatter and 27 MB*.
   Separation in structure: in-edges equal out-edges for 97% of pairs, so store only the reverse **weights** and
   the 3% difference.
2. *Dijkstra settles one vertex at a time* ↔ *the hardware wants 10 or more outstanding misses per core*.
   Separation in space: independent queries have no ordering constraint between them.
3. *Every phase needs large fresh buffers* ↔ *first touch costs a fault plus zeroing, and munmap costs TLB
   shootdowns across 12 threads*. Use the harmful resource: the 40 MB bucket region is dead after placement, which
   is exactly when the copy and the transpose need 27 MB each.

## 3. Three approaches that have not been tested

### A. Representation: one merged bidirectional adjacency, no reverse CSR

- **Mechanism.** Keep the renumbered forward CSR. Add a per-edge reverse weight, `bw[e]` = min w(v→u) for forward
  edge e = u→v (absent if there is none), plus a small side CSR for the 144k in-only edges. Backward expansion of x
  reads forward row x with `bw` and then x's side list. The transpose (count, prefix and scatter, with atomics) is
  replaced by one pass that finds u in row v. After renumbering, v is close to u, so the lookup is local, has no
  atomics and writes sequentially.
- **Why it is new.** The Nov 1 researcher noted FlyHigh's single structure but rated it "≥10 h, full rewrite", and
  the synthesis dropped it. Step 22 fused the transpose into the copy with atomics, which is a different mechanism.
  The minimal form here leaves the forward row format, the parser, the BFS and the copy unchanged.
- **Evidence.**
  - **M (ours, new):** 5.14M merged entries against 10M today. Only 144k side entries.
  - **M (FlyHigh, on the box, their code; a lead, not evidence for ours):** queries 80 → 56–65 ms; the transpose,
    33 ms with atomics, became a 17 ms reverse-weight pass. Their single board run moved only 0.196 → 0.191 s.
- **Size estimate (D).** Reverse CSR (20 MB of edges + 7 MB of offsets) → `bw` as u16 (10 MB) + side list
  (≈0.6 MB). Search structure 54 → ≈38 MB. The process peak (78.8 MB, at load placement) does not move, so ΔM ≈ 0
  unless H5 (6-byte buckets) is also done.
- **Synergy.** The bypass was rejected because classification needed in-edge information inside an existing pass
  (step 28 README). With `bw` that classification is a local O(row) test.
- **Expected benefit (S).** Build −5 to −10 ms on the Mac at 12 threads (transpose is 15.6 ms). Search 0 to −20%,
  larger on the box than on the Mac. Total −5 to −15%, about +0.6 to +1.8 points. S +40–60 lines (≈ −0.25 points).
- **Correctness risks.**
  - Parallel reverse edges: `bw` must take the min over every match in row v.
  - Forward duplicates carry the same `bw`. That is harmless but must not be deduplicated wrongly.
  - Self-loops: skip them.
  - Weight 0 must stay distinct from "absent".
  - Escapes: u16 `bw` needs codes for absent and for heavy, with a heavy lookup keyed by the edge index.
  - Degree rejection must count `bw` entries plus side entries.
  - The near-2^32 spur and no-in edges are exactly the rare escaped cases. Trap: each of the 17 no-in nodes has
    2 out-edges weighing [2^32 − 2^20, 2^32 − 1] with no back-edge. So those 34 edges are both **one-way and
    heavy**, they land in the side list, and **the side CSR needs its own escape path**, not just `bw`.
  - Tests: every fixture, and the all-pairs random reference (mu-prune style) with heavy one-way and parallel
    density.
  - Mutants that must be caught:
    - drop the side list;
    - drop the side-list escape (caught by the "from no-in node" query, answer ≥ 4,293,918,720).
- **Cheapest discriminating experiment (≈1.5 h).** Phase-timer diagnostic build that computes `bw` and the side CSR
  **beside** the existing transpose. Time both on the Mac at 12 threads and on the VPS, and check that
  `bw`/side match the transpose's in-edge multiset (min weight per pair). Prediction: the reverse-weight pass costs
  ≤ 50% of the transpose. **Reject A** if it saves < 5 ms on the Mac at 12 threads. Only then switch the backward
  search over (≈2 h) and measure the search phase.

### B. Execution: more outstanding misses in the search (cross-query interleaving)

- **Mechanism.** Each worker runs k = 2–4 independent queries and alternates one settle step between them, so k
  dependency chains have misses in flight at once (AMAC-style interleaving: Kocberber et al., VLDB 2015). Each query
  keeps its exact heap order, so per-query semantics are unchanged. An alternative inside one query is to prefetch
  the rows and labels of the top 2–4 heap entries instead of only the top entry (current code,
  `main.rs:667–668`).
- **Why it is not the closed queue question.** Dial ×3, radix and PGO changed queue arithmetic with one expansion in
  flight. This changes memory-level parallelism. Our own 1-ahead prefetch gave −7 to −13% (**M**), which shows that
  latency, not arithmetic, sets the cost.
- **Evidence needed.** Spare MLP on the box. **D** The board already runs 12 threads on 6 SMT cores (2 chains per
  core). The M1 has no SMT, so **the Mac will overstate the gain**. The VPS is unclear (vCPU-to-thread topology
  unknown).
- **Expected benefit (S).** Search −10 to −25% on the box, total −4 to −11%. Memory: k × the per-worker label
  pages during the search phase. That is below the load peak only if k × 12 × (largest search) fits; measure it.
  S +30–50.
- **Correctness risks.** Low: queries are independent, and the shared u64 fallback must stay per query.
  Answer-slot bookkeeping is the main source of bugs.
- **Cheapest discriminating experiment (≈2 h).** k = 2 in a scratch build. Search phase only, at 1 thread and at
  the full worker count, on both the Mac and the VPS. Prediction: +20% per-thread throughput at 1 thread. **Reject
  B** if the VPS gain at 12 workers is < 8% (SMT already supplies the MLP). Inconclusive if the gap between the Mac
  and the VPS is large; then only the board can decide.

### C. Systems: page faults, zeroing, munmap and spawns as a hidden share of board T

- **Mechanism.** About 190 MB is first-touched per run (48k minor faults, **M** VPS) on 4 KB pages, with THP off
  for the whole process. `mallopt(M_MMAP_THRESHOLD, 256 KB)` turns every large `Vec` into an mmap/munmap pair. Each
  munmap issues TLB-shootdown IPIs to every core that ran the process. 84 thread spawns each map a 2 MB stack and
  take `mmap_lock` for writing, which stalls faulting siblings. The fix: one pre-sized arena reused across phases
  (the bucket region becomes the copy and transpose buffers) and one persistent pool of 12 threads with barriers.
- **Why it is new.** Step 22's buffer reuse covered the transpose only and used atomics (peak 85 MB). E1 attacked
  faults through THP, which carries a compaction-stall risk under the board's `defrag` policy (the likely cause of
  its board loss, untested). This approach removes faults without THP.
- **Evidence.**
  - **M** E1 on the VPS: −24% minor faults gave −4.7 to −7.1% wall time.
  - **M (Cache Me, their PERF; a lead):** a persistent pool moved the board 0.183/0.196 → 0.172/0.168 s.
  - **M (FlyHigh, box):** THP off for the whole process cost 0.196 → 0.223 s (+14%). That is the same direction:
    page-level overhead is large on the box.
  - **U** Our sys and fault share on the board.
- **Expected benefit (S).** −3 to −8% T. ΔM ≤ 0, since reuse can lower the copy-phase peak. S +20–40.
- **Correctness risks.** Logic risk is low. Lifetime and aliasing mistakes in unsafe reuse are the main risk.
  Barrier deadlocks with 0 queries or 0 edges.
- **Cheapest discriminating experiment (≈20 min, partly free with Experiment 0).**
  1. On the VPS, `measure.rs` already records rusage. Compare our sys time and minor faults with a competitor
     binary at the same T scale.
  2. Microbenchmark: 12 threads first-touch 190 MB with 4 KB pages, versus the same memory touched a second time.
     The difference bounds C on the VPS.
  **Reject C** if the bound is < 10 ms at 12 workers, or if our sys share is no higher than FlyHigh's or
  HighVelocity's.

## 6. Experiment 0 results (2026-11-02, 06:00 UTC; VPS measurement)

Evidence: [plateau-model README](../tests/performance/2026-11-02-track-d-plateau-model/README.md). Scored code unchanged.

**Measured (M):**
1. **The VPS is a faithful proxy for large differences.** VPS/board ratios to ours at 6 workers: Cache Me
   0.82/0.84, HighVelocity 0.58/0.56, FlyHigh 0.82/0.76. The "blind proxy" explanation of the plateau is
   **rejected**. The earlier SWAR and THP mismatches were effects below board noise.
2. **The gap is user CPU.** HighVelocity uses 1.28 against our 2.21 CPU-s, while the sys difference is 0.08
   CPU-s. **C is rejected as a primary lever.** At most it is worth a few percent.
3. Our gap to HighVelocity with its bypass off, by phase (wall ms, 6 workers):

   | Phase | Gap (ms) | Share of the 165 ms gap |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | Search | +76 | 46% |
   | Renumber | +32 | 19% |
   | Reverse graph | +26 | 16% |
   | Load | +20 | 12% |

   HighVelocity's out-degree bypass adds another −10% of total time.
4. **HighVelocity's search does the same work** (7.52M expansions, 22.2M relaxations; ours 7.57M settles), at
   84–97 ns CPU per expansion against our 147. That is 1.5–1.75× at equal work, compared across sessions. The
   4-ahead prefetch accounts for ≈20% of that advantage ((730 − 631) / (1113 − 631)). The remaining 1.5× at equal work is unattributed among the bucket-ring FIFO drain,
   4-slot inline rows (one cache line per row, no offsets array), branch-free relaxation and u64 labels in
   128-vertex pages.

**Deductions (D):**
- The plateau came from optimizing the wrong layer. Steps 20–28 targeted work (μ-prune, bypass) or queue
  arithmetic in isolation. The measured 1.5–1.75× is a per-expansion constant at equal work, so it comes from the
  combination of queue, layout and memory-level parallelism in one engine. Our Dial attempts (step 21) were queue swaps alone; they kept the heap-era
  layout and expansion loop.
- HighVelocity's bypass rule needs out-degree only, which is known while rows are placed. Step 28 concluded that
  in-edge information was required. That was a false constraint, imposed by our symmetric (in = out = {a, b}) rule.

**Revised ranking (S: estimates against the VPS 0.50 s; the board scales by ≈0.4):**

| Rank | Mechanism | Measured ceiling (from the HV diff) | Est. ΔT for us | Effort | Key risk |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Search engine: per-vertex inline rows, bucket ring with FIFO drain, 4-ahead row/label prefetch, branch-free relaxation | 76 ms (−15%) | −8 to −15% | 4–5 h | Partial ports have failed before (Dial ×3). The gain may need the whole combination. |
| 2 | Out-degree bypass inside placement and the BFS walk | −10% (HV's own ablation) | −6 to −10% | 4–6 h | Exactness of the one-way walk rule (HV's `steps < 64` and stop-at-branch logic) |
| 3 | A: reverse weights or an owner-computes transpose without atomics | 26 ms (−5%) | −4 to −6% | 2–3 h | Side-list escapes (see §3A) |
